# v0.5 — one question set, asked of every translation

**Released:** 2026-07 · **Changes from:** [v0.4](v0.4.md)

Every translation now faces the **identical** set of references. Previously each
language drew its own verses against its own primary edition, so two columns of
the board answered different questions — and English answered 357 of them while
every other language answered 55.

## The one idea

A translation is a translation. There is no per-language anything: no
`english_count`, no `other_language_count`, no `lang == "eng"` branch anywhere in
the sampler.

One reference list is drawn per benchmark version and asked of all 18 editions:

| tier | references | how |
|---|---|---|
| famous | 63 | curated, `dataset/famous-v1.jsonl` |
| obscure | 20 | curated, `dataset/obscure-v1.jsonl` |
| body | 66 | exactly one verse per book of the shared 66, seeded by benchmark version |
| **total** | **149** | × 18 translations ≈ **2,700 items** |

Body being one-verse-per-book is what makes the tier comparable: every book
weighs the same regardless of length, and every edition is asked the same verse
of Genesis, the same verse of Exodus, and so on. The draw needs no API call —
chapter and verse bounds come from the vendored `eng` versification table.

## Versification correction

References are drawn in the `eng` scheme and **translated into each edition's own
scheme** before being asked. This is not optional. The Psalter is renumbered
wholesale in the `lxx` and `rso` schemes:

```
ARA  (eng) PSA.23.1   O Senhor é o meu pastor; nada me faltará.
AVM  (lxx) PSA.22.1   O Senhor é meu pastor, nada me faltará.     <- corrected
AVM  (lxx) PSA.23.1   Do Senhor é a terra e tudo o que ela contém  <- naive: Psalm 24
```

Three of the eighteen editions use those schemes. Uncorrected, they would be asked
a different psalm and graded as if they had failed. Mappings are the Paratext
rules, vendored in `dataset/versification/`; each edition's scheme comes from the
Core API's own `vrs` field. This is also why the four Psalms references dropped
from `famous` in v0.4 are **restored** — correction makes them safe.

## Absent references drop

Where an edition doesn't carry the resolved reference, the item is dropped.
Asking an edition for a verse it lacks is Hallucination Resistance's subject
(`absent_from_version`), not this dimension's.

145 of the 149 references are present in all 18 editions. The 9 drops across 5
editions are all explained:

* `DAN.1.2`, `EST.9.20` — editions carrying the **Greek** Daniel and Esther
  address them as `DAG`/`ESG`, so they have no `DAN`/`EST` at all. Book identity,
  not versification: those books exist in two textual forms.
* `NEH.3.3` → `EZR.13.3` in `lxx`, which treats Ezra–Nehemiah as one book. Correct
  per the mapping; AVM addresses Nehemiah separately.
* `1PE.1.25` in AVM — a genuine verse merge.

Consequence: five editions get no Daniel or Esther body item. A `DAN↔DAG` /
`EST↔ESG` equivalence would recover it and is deliberately not implemented — it is
a second concept, and the drop is honest.

## The `edge` tier is gone

It was measured across 7 runs and probed no distinct weakness:

| tier | pooled mean | fabrication rate |
|---|---|---|
| famous | 79.3 | 9.1% |
| body | 67.2 | 20.0% |
| obscure | **63.7** | **24.4%** |
| ~~edge~~ | ~~69.3~~ | ~~16.8%~~ |

Edge scored **above** body in 7 of 7 runs and fabricated *less*. Its grade
distribution tracked body's, and it wasn't an artefact of verse length (120 chars
against body's 125). Chapter-final verses turn out to be short doxologies, and
single-chapter books are Obadiah, Philemon, Jude and 2–3 John — short and heavily
quoted. It was also the one tier whose property ("the final verse of a chapter")
could not be held constant across versification schemes.

The genuine edge is **obscure**, the hardest tier on every run, so edge's budget
moved there: 10 → 20 references.

## Not comparable with v0.4

Every Direct Quotation item changed, so v0.4 and v0.5 headline scores measure
different question sets. v0.4 results stay frozen at v0.4 and the board shows one
generation at a time.
