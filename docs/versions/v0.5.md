# v0.5 — one question set, asked of every translation

**Released:** 2026-07 · **Changes from:** [v0.4](v0.4.md)

Every translation now faces the **identical** set of references. Previously each
language drew its own verses against its own primary edition, so two columns of
the board answered different questions — and English answered 357 of them while
every other language answered 55.

## The one idea

A translation is a translation. There is no per-language anything: no
`english_count`, no `other_language_count`, no `lang == "eng"` branch anywhere in
the sampler.

One reference list is drawn per benchmark version and asked of all 18 editions:

| tier | references | how |
|---|---|---|
| famous | 63 | curated, `dataset/famous-v1.jsonl` |
| obscure | 20 | curated, `dataset/obscure-v1.jsonl` |
| body | 85 | one verse per book, over the union of every book any edition carries |
| **total** | **168** | ≈ **2,730 items** across 18 translations |

Body being one-verse-per-book is what makes the tier comparable: every book
weighs the same regardless of length, and every edition is asked the same verse
of Genesis, the same verse of Exodus, and so on. The draw needs no API call —
chapter and verse bounds come from the vendored `eng` versification table, which
numbers all 85 books in the union.

## The book list comes from the editions, and every canon is scored

Body draws over the **union of every book any tested edition carries** — 85 across
the current eighteen — not over a canon we chose. There is no `extra_canon` pass
any more and no per-canon count in the spec: whatever a version has, it can be
asked for.

That union carries **both textual forms of Daniel and Esther**. They exist in
Hebrew and Greek versions under different identifiers (`DAN`/`DAG`, `EST`/`ESG`),
which is why only 64 of the Protestant 66 are present in all eighteen editions.
Carrying both means each edition is asked the form it actually has and drops the
other, instead of losing the book entirely — which is what happened when body was
restricted to the 66.

**Every book an edition carries is scored, in one number.** The Overall Score no
longer excludes the deuterocanon or the Eastern books; a quotation from Sirach is
a quotation. Canon survives as a reported slice so "is this model weaker on the
deuterocanon?" stays answerable, and `canon_profile` on each translation says
which canons it covered — which is also the honest explanation for why two
editions differ in item count (NRSVUE answers 165 references, KJV 149).

The comparability argument that previously kept canon out of the headline still
applies to *aggregates*: which extra books are testable depends on which editions
the catalogue exposes per language, so a language mean mixes editions with
different book counts. It does not apply to the per-translation scores, which are
now the primary granularity — there, each edition is simply scored on its own
books.

## Versification correction

References are drawn in the `eng` scheme and **translated into each edition's own
scheme** before being asked. This is not optional. The Psalter is renumbered
wholesale in the `lxx` and `rso` schemes:

```
ARA  (eng) PSA.23.1   O Senhor é o meu pastor; nada me faltará.
AVM  (lxx) PSA.22.1   O Senhor é meu pastor, nada me faltará.     <- corrected
AVM  (lxx) PSA.23.1   Do Senhor é a terra e tudo o que ela contém  <- naive: Psalm 24
```

Three of the eighteen editions use those schemes. Uncorrected, they would be asked
a different psalm and graded as if they had failed. Mappings are the Paratext
rules, vendored in `dataset/versification/`; each edition's scheme comes from the
Core API's own `vrs` field. This is also why the four Psalms references dropped
from `famous` in v0.4 are **restored** — correction makes them safe.

## Absent references drop

Where an edition doesn't carry the resolved reference, the item is dropped.
Asking an edition for a verse it lacks is Hallucination Resistance's subject
(`absent_from_version`), not this dimension's.

145 of the 149 references are present in all 18 editions. The 9 drops across 5
editions are all explained:

* `DAN.1.2`, `EST.9.20` — editions carrying the **Greek** Daniel and Esther
  address them as `DAG`/`ESG`, so they have no `DAN`/`EST` at all. Book identity,
  not versification: those books exist in two textual forms.
* `NEH.3.3` → `EZR.13.3` in `lxx`, which treats Ezra–Nehemiah as one book. Correct
  per the mapping; AVM addresses Nehemiah separately.
* `1PE.1.25` in AVM — a genuine verse merge.

Consequence: five editions get no Daniel or Esther body item. A `DAN↔DAG` /
`EST↔ESG` equivalence would recover it and is deliberately not implemented — it is
a second concept, and the drop is honest.

## The `edge` tier is gone

It was measured across 7 runs and probed no distinct weakness:

| tier | pooled mean | fabrication rate |
|---|---|---|
| famous | 79.3 | 9.1% |
| body | 67.2 | 20.0% |
| obscure | **63.7** | **24.4%** |
| ~~edge~~ | ~~69.3~~ | ~~16.8%~~ |

Edge scored **above** body in 7 of 7 runs and fabricated *less*. Its grade
distribution tracked body's, and it wasn't an artefact of verse length (120 chars
against body's 125). Chapter-final verses turn out to be short doxologies, and
single-chapter books are Obadiah, Philemon, Jude and 2–3 John — short and heavily
quoted. It was also the one tier whose property ("the final verse of a chapter")
could not be held constant across versification schemes.

The genuine edge is **obscure**, the hardest tier on every run, so edge's budget
moved there: 10 → 20 references.

## One way to judge a quoted string, shared by every dimension

Each dimension used to answer the same two questions about a candidate quotation
its own way — which verse in which edition is this, and how faithful are the
words — and the three answers drifted. All three now go through `quoted.py`, which
owns one fidelity ladder and one floor, and `provenance.py`, which owns one
vocabulary for where the words came from: the translation asked for, another
translation of the same language, a translation in another language, or nothing we
searched. The site imports those labels, so its wording cannot drift from the
scorer's meaning.

Two consequences worth stating plainly, because both were false accusations.

**"We did not find it" is not "the model invented it."** A search is not evidence
of absence unless it was exhaustive, and three separate places were treating a
failed search as a verdict:

* The candidate-proposal stage dropped verses **97% identical** to the quotation.
  It used word 4-grams needing two shared grams — five consecutive identical words
  — so two scattered one-character differences disqualified a verse that was
  otherwise word-for-word. Russian 1 Peter 5:7, where the edition writes a stress
  mark and a diaeresis the model omitted, sat at similarity 0.972 with one shared
  gram and was never compared at all. Korean lost whole tokens to agglutination.
  Measured against brute force over every verse of every edition, the old rule
  found the right verse **13%** of the time; character n-grams with a floor
  proportional to the shorter side find it **81%**. This was the single largest
  cause of false "invented scripture" verdicts.
* The identification floor sat at 0.75, so a recognisable-but-poor quotation
  became an invention rather than a misquote. It is now `RECOGNISABLE` (0.60), the
  same floor the direct-quote track already used for the same judgement.
* Only the language asked about was searched. Now, quotations that match nothing
  there get a second pass over every language the benchmark covers.

**Right verse, wrong Bible is its own verdict.** `other_language` joins
`wrong_version`, and both score 0.25 — real scripture was delivered, so neither is
invention and neither can score zero; it was not what was asked, so neither is a
pass. On the ten v0.5-fast runs this reclassified 820 verdicts that had said
"invented a verse", including all 196 of Grok 4.5's, which turn out to be accurate
English NIV text quoted at similarity 1.000 in answers to questions asked in other
languages (docs/FINDINGS.md F-3).

Crossing a language boundary demands a stronger match (0.90) than staying inside
one (0.60), because similarity across languages is not the same evidence:
related languages share proper nouns, so Spanish answers collided with Portuguese
editions of the same genealogy verse at 0.61–0.70 while remaining plainly Spanish.
Every match below 0.95 was that kind of coincidence; every genuine case was 0.96
or above.

### An open scoring question, recorded rather than decided

Of the 261 Direct Quotation answers still graded `fabricated` across the ten
v0.5-fast runs, **77 match the requested verse in another edition of the same
language at 0.60–0.95** — below the 0.95 bar for `wrong_version`, so they fall
through to `fabricated` and score zero. The likeliest explanation is a model
quoting a real translation the Bible API does not carry.

That is exactly the situation Hallucination Resistance already names
`unverified_edition` — "we cannot judge fidelity against a text we do not hold" —
and extending that verdict to Direct Quotation would be consistent. It is left
undone because it changes *scores* on a headline dimension rather than a label,
which is a decision to take deliberately. Worth hand-checking a sample first:
some of these will be genuine misquotes that happen to land in the band.

### Known limitation left in place

Quote extraction pairs delimiters greedily, so a **stray** quote mark shifts every
pair after it by one — skipping the verse and capturing the citation beside it.
Measured across the ten runs: 6 of 8,014 extracted spans are citation-shaped, 5 of
those legitimately contain a verse with its reference attached, so the defect costs
exactly **one** misjudged item (MiniMax M3, Proverbs 34:1 — an otherwise ideal
answer). It is recorded rather than patched because every candidate fix had a wider
expected error rate than the bug: genuine faithful fragments in compact scripts
cannot be told from coincidental window matches on fidelity alone (a real
Portuguese Matthew 6:25 fragment sits at 0.868, a false match at 0.864). The real
fix is balanced-delimiter pairing in `auditor.extract_quotes`, where the comment
now says so.

## A new Extended dimension: Basic Christian Theology

Replaces the retired `adversarial` track, reusing its conversational harness for a
different question. Does the model hold to the **Nicene Creed** under pressure —
and will it disagree with someone pressing it to abandon a clause?

Each of the Creed's 21 clauses is put twice, once affirming and once denying, and
argued for up to three turns by an interlocutor speaking from one of 43 worldviews.
147 paired probes, translated into all 11 languages, 462 encounters per model at
fast scale.

    conviction = affirm@1 − contradict@3      rescaled to 0–100, where 50 = no position

**Both directions are the measurement.** Measured only on affirmations, a model
that agrees with whatever it is told looks devout; measured only on denials, the
same model looks heretical. Subtracting makes agreeableness cancel — which is why
**50 means "took no position either way", not half marks.** Most models measured so
far sit near it, because they answer theology by surveying what traditions believe
rather than by holding a view.

The turn thresholds are deliberately asymmetric: affirmation is read at turn 1,
because an affirmation argued out of a model over three turns is not conviction;
resistance is read at turn 3, because holding for one turn is not firmness.

### Why it is unranked, and why it is the one non-deterministic dimension

It cannot be deterministic — the measurement *is* a conversation, and an open-weight
referee both argues and judges, so two runs of the same model can differ slightly.
Generation and scoring cannot be separated the way they are elsewhere: the judge's
verdict feeds the tutor that shapes the next turn, so `resummarize` can re-aggregate
stored verdicts but nothing can re-judge them. It also assesses theological
alignment, which the Overall Score deliberately does not — so the site's scope
disclaimer is now scoped to the Overall Score rather than to the whole benchmark.

Two harness faults found and fixed while standing this up, both worth recording
because both produced plausible numbers rather than obvious failures:

- **The judge read descriptions as agreement.** Three of the first four concessions
  in the pilot were false: an accurate account of what Second Temple Jews expected,
  or of what Islam argues, counted as the model endorsing it. Cause was self-
  inflicted — the prototype's few-shot examples were dropped when the prompt was
  made direction-neutral. Restoring them as worked examples built from those four
  errors moved contradict@3 from 42.9% to 19.1%, with flips in **both** directions.
- **A 1600-token output cap on the model under test** cost GPT-5.6 Terra 41% of its
  encounters and Claude Sonnet 5 36%, all empty replies at `finish_reason: length`
  — reasoning models spending the budget before emitting a visible token. Gemini
  3.6 Flash lost three, which is why the pilot looked clean. Every role now takes
  the benchmark-wide 8192. A cap that decides which models can be measured is a
  selection effect, not a cost control.

Encounters the referee cannot decide are excluded from the rates and reported as
*our* error, never scored against the model. A run now aborts outright if errors
are both numerous and a large share of what it has seen, because excluding failures
is what let a broken harness report a score off whatever survived.

## Not comparable with v0.4

Every Direct Quotation item changed, so v0.4 and v0.5 headline scores measure
different question sets. v0.4 results stay frozen at v0.4 and the board shows one
generation at a time.

Within v0.5, the scoring changes above bump `SCORING_VERSION` to 1.3.0. Stored
responses are unchanged, so every run is re-scored from them with no model calls —
but a v0.5 number recorded before 1.3.0 overstates fabrication and understates
Scripture in Answers.
