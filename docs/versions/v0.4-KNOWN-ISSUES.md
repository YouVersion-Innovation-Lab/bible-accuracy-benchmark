# v0.4 — open issue blocking publication

**Status:** diagnosed, not fixed. Two v0.4 runs are complete and re-scored but
**must not be published** until this is resolved, because it penalises some models
several times harder than others.

## Symptom

After the scoring fixes in `597e3b2`, Scripture in Answers still records large
numbers of `fabricated` quotations — text presented as scripture that matched no
verse in any translation — and the rate differs enormously by model:

| Model | topical quotations | `fabricated` | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini 3.6 Flash | 5,725 | 163 | 2.8% |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | 7,981 | 1,236 | **15.5%** |

A 5× gap in apparent invention is not credible as a model difference, and it maps
onto answer *length* rather than accuracy: Sonnet writes long, multi-verse
answers.

## The detector is not the problem

Given a single verse and a single span, detection is correct:

| Span (from a real Sonnet answer) | Result in isolation |
|---|---|
| `and wine to gladden the heart of man` | ESV Psalm 104:15, similarity **1.000** |
| `for the just shall live by faith` | KJV Romans 1:17, similarity **0.951** |
| `do not be drunk with wine in which is debauchery` | no match — correct, it is the model's own condensation of Ephesians 5:18, not any edition's wording |

The first two were nevertheless recorded as `fabricated` in the run.

## Cause

`VersionIndex.present()` is **verse-driven over the whole response**. For each
candidate verse it computes one alignment:

```python
aln = fuzz.partial_ratio_alignment(vloose, loose_response)
```

That aligns the **entire verse** against the **entire response** and yields at most
**one window per verse**. Two consequences:

1. **Competing text wins the window.** When a model quotes a short fragment of a
   long verse inside a long answer, the best whole-verse alignment can land
   somewhere other than the fragment. The window is then the wrong region, the
   similarity computed against it is low, and the verse is dropped — even though
   the fragment matches it perfectly.
2. **One detection per verse per response.** `scan_responses` returns
   `{usfm: best Detection}`, so a verse quoted twice can only be credited once.
   The second occurrence has no detection overlapping its marked span and falls to
   the `fabricated` branch. Measured contribution: 41 of 1,236 (3%) — real, but
   not the main cause.

Longer answers contain more competing text, so the failure scales with verbosity.
That is why it looks like a model difference.

## Fix

Identify **marked spans by their own content** rather than inferring them from
whole-response verse alignment. The machinery already exists and is unused on this
path: `quotefind.Span` and `quotefind.identify_all` take explicit spans and
identify each one independently, which is exactly the right shape here.

Proposed split:

- **Marked spans** (quotation marks / blockquote): identify each span with
  `identify_all`. Deterministic, no window guessing, and unaffected by answer
  length or repetition.
- **Unmarked text**: keep the current verse-driven `present()` pass, which is the
  right tool when there are no boundaries to work from.

Then a verse quoted twice yields two verdicts, and a fragment is judged on the
words the model actually put in quotes.

## Also worth revisiting when this is fixed

`fabricated` on this track is doing double duty. "Matched no verse anywhere" covers
both genuine invention and the model's own loose condensation of a real verse — the
Ephesians 5:18 case above is the latter, and calling it invention overstates it.
Once span-level identification lands, a span that best-matches a real verse below
the accuracy floor should be a `misquote`, leaving `fabricated` for text that
resembles no verse at all.
